Objective & scenario alignment

Horizon Gulf Bank (HGB) is a fictional UAE retail bank with 250,000 customers. Its Board approved Project MIZAN — an AI decisioning platform covering credit, fraud, collateral valuation, cash logistics, customer retention and document automation. You are a junior ML practitioner in the MIZAN squad.

The Retail Marketing team runs one-size-fits-all campaigns. They want 4–7 actionable segments with clear personas so that product offers, channel and retention budget can be tailored. There is no label — this is unsupervised learning.

Progressive architecture: uses your customers_clean.parquet; segments become an enrichment feature and a monitoring dimension later.

By the end of this lab you will be able to

  • Select and transform behavioural features for distance-based clustering
  • Scale k-means to 250k rows with MiniBatchKMeans
  • Choose k with inertia (elbow) and silhouette on a sample
  • Profile and name clusters; check churn by segment after clustering
  • Cross-check with Ward hierarchical clustering (adjusted Rand index) and visualise with PCA

1Select and transform behavioural features 8 min

Why this matters

Segments should describe behaviour the bank can act on — balances, engagement, product holding — not protected attributes. Money variables are log-transformed (otherwise a few millionaires form their own clusters), then everything is standardised so each feature contributes equally to distance.

Your tasks

  1. Load the clean customers. Build B with: log balance, log salary, tenure, num_products, digital logins, branch visits, card-spend-to-salary ratio, log days since last transaction, complaints.
  2. Standardise into Xs. Print the mean and std of each column (≈ 0 / 1).
Checkpoint — you should see

All means ≈ 0 and stds ≈ 1.

🔑 Reference solution (Level 3 code)
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler
cust = pd.read_parquet(CKPT / "customers_clean.parquet")
B = pd.DataFrame({
    "log_balance": np.log1p(cust.avg_balance_aed), "log_salary": np.log1p(cust.monthly_salary_aed),
    "tenure_months": cust.tenure_months, "num_products": cust.num_products,
    "digital_logins_30d": cust.digital_logins_30d, "branch_visits_90d": cust.branch_visits_90d,
    "card_spend_ratio": cust.card_spend_monthly_aed / cust.monthly_salary_aed,
    "log_days_since_txn": np.log1p(cust.days_since_last_txn), "complaints_12m": cust.complaints_12m})
Xs = StandardScaler().fit_transform(B)
pd.DataFrame(Xs, columns=B.columns).agg(["mean", "std"]).round(2)
Think like a practitioner

Why are gender, nationality_group and age not in the clustering features? Could you still describe segments by them afterwards?

2Choose k with elbow and silhouette 10 min

Why this matters

MiniBatchKMeans fits on mini-batches, making 250k rows fast. Silhouette is O(n²), so we score it on a 15k-row sample. We look for a k where inertia’s improvement slows and silhouette is still reasonable — within the 4–7 range Marketing can operationalise.

Your tasks

  1. For k = 2…9 fit MiniBatchKMeans(n_clusters=k, batch_size=4096, n_init=5, random_state=RNG) on Xs; record inertia and silhouette (15k sample).
  2. Plot both; choose K (the best silhouette among k = 4…7).
Checkpoint — you should see

Inertia falls smoothly; silhouette only ≈ 0.10–0.18 (typical for behavioural data). K = 4 is selected.

🔑 Reference solution (Level 3 code)
from sklearn.cluster import MiniBatchKMeans
from sklearn.metrics import silhouette_score
idx = np.random.default_rng(RNG).choice(len(Xs), 15_000, replace=False)
rows = []
for k in range(2, 10):
    km = MiniBatchKMeans(n_clusters=k, batch_size=4096, n_init=5, random_state=RNG).fit(Xs)
    rows.append((k, km.inertia_, silhouette_score(Xs[idx], km.labels_[idx])))
sel = pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["k", "inertia", "silhouette"]).set_index("k")
sel.plot(subplots=True, layout=(1, 2), marker="o", figsize=(11, 3.8)); plt.show()
K = int(sel.loc[4:7, "silhouette"].idxmax())
print("chosen K =", K); sel.round(3)
Think like a practitioner

A silhouette of 0.15 is ‘weak structure’. Is the segmentation useless? (No — segments can be useful for action even when boundaries are fuzzy.)

3Fit, profile and name the segments 12 min

Why this matters

Clusters are only valuable once a human can describe them. A profile in original units plus a z-score heat-map makes the personas obvious. Churn was not used for clustering, so churn by segment is an honest test of business relevance.

Your tasks

  1. Fit the final MiniBatchKMeans(K) and add segment to cust.
  2. Build profile: size share, medians of the behavioural features, churn rate per segment.
  3. Heat-map of cluster-centre z-scores. Write a persona dictionary PERSONA = {segment: 'name'} from what you see.
Checkpoint — you should see

Recognisable groups emerge, e.g. digital high-earners, dormant low-balance, new-to-bank, branch-loyal long-tenure. Churn differs by 2–4× between segments.

💡 Hint

Rename the automatic personas to marketing-friendly names in your own notebook — e.g. ‘Affluent Digital’, ‘At-risk Dormant’.

🔑 Reference solution (Level 3 code)
km = MiniBatchKMeans(n_clusters=K, batch_size=4096, n_init=10, random_state=RNG).fit(Xs)
cust["segment"] = km.labels_
profile = cust.assign(**B).groupby("segment").agg(share=("customer_id", lambda s: len(s) / len(cust)),
    salary=("monthly_salary_aed", "median"), balance=("avg_balance_aed", "median"), tenure=("tenure_months", "median"),
    products=("num_products", "mean"), logins=("digital_logins_30d", "mean"), branch=("branch_visits_90d", "mean"),
    days_idle=("days_since_last_txn", "median"), churn_rate=("churned_6m", "mean"))
display(profile.round(2))
centres = pd.DataFrame(km.cluster_centers_, columns=B.columns)
plt.figure(figsize=(11, 3.5)); plt.imshow(centres, cmap="RdBu_r", vmin=-1.5, vmax=1.5, aspect="auto")
plt.xticks(range(B.shape[1]), B.columns, rotation=35, ha="right"); plt.yticks(range(K)); plt.colorbar(label="z-score"); plt.title("Segment centres"); plt.show()
PERSONA = {k: " / ".join(f"{'high' if centres.loc[k, c] > 0 else 'low'} {c}" for c in centres.loc[k].abs().nlargest(2).index) for k in range(K)}
cust["persona"] = cust.segment.map(PERSONA); PERSONA

4Cross-check with hierarchical clustering and PCA 10 min

Why this matters

If a different algorithm finds similar groups, the structure is real rather than an artefact of k-means. Ward linkage on a sample and the adjusted Rand index (0 = chance, 1 = identical) quantify the agreement. PCA gives a 2-D picture of the segments.

Your tasks

  1. On a 4,000-row sample run Ward linkage, plot a truncated dendrogram, cut it into K clusters with fcluster.
  2. Compute adjusted_rand_score against the k-means labels of the same rows.
  3. Project a 20k sample to 2 principal components and scatter-plot coloured by segment; print explained variance.
Checkpoint — you should see

ARI ≈ 0.3–0.6 (moderate agreement); PCA shows overlapping but distinct regions.

🔑 Reference solution (Level 3 code)
from scipy.cluster.hierarchy import linkage, dendrogram, fcluster
from sklearn.metrics import adjusted_rand_score
from sklearn.decomposition import PCA
h_idx = idx[:4000]
Z = linkage(Xs[h_idx], method="ward")
plt.figure(figsize=(12, 3.5)); dendrogram(Z, truncate_mode="lastp", p=25); plt.title("Ward dendrogram (4k sample)"); plt.show()
h_lab = fcluster(Z, t=K, criterion="maxclust")
print("adjusted Rand index (Ward vs k-means):", round(adjusted_rand_score(km.labels_[h_idx], h_lab), 3))
pca = PCA(n_components=2, random_state=RNG).fit(Xs)
P2 = pca.transform(Xs[idx[:20000]])
plt.scatter(P2[:, 0], P2[:, 1], c=km.labels_[idx[:20000]], s=2, cmap="tab10")
plt.xlabel("PC1"); plt.ylabel("PC2"); plt.title(f"Segments in PCA space ({pca.explained_variance_ratio_.sum():.0%} variance shown)"); plt.show()
Think like a practitioner

Only ~35% of variance is visible in 2-D. Why might clusters that overlap in the PCA plot still be well separated?

5Persist segments for downstream use 5 min

Why this matters

Segments become a feature for other models and a dimension for monitoring (e.g. fraud or churn rate by segment). We save the mapping and the fitted scaler + model so that new customers can be assigned consistently.

Your tasks

  1. Save customer_id, segment, persona to CKPT/customer_segments.parquet.
  2. Assert every customer has exactly one segment.
Checkpoint — you should see

Segment shares match the profile table; two files saved.

🔑 Reference solution (Level 3 code)
import joblib
seg = cust[["customer_id", "segment", "persona"]]
assert seg.customer_id.is_unique and seg.segment.notna().all()

Stretch challenges

  1. Replace k-means with a GaussianMixture. Which customers have uncertain membership (max probability < 0.6)?
  2. Try DBSCAN on a 20k sample. What does it label as noise, and is that useful (outlier customers)?
  3. Track segment migration: re-run in 6 months and compute a transition matrix between old and new segments.

Troubleshooting

  • Silhouette slow → keep the 15k sample.
  • Different K than your neighbour → MiniBatchKMeans is stochastic; fix random_state.
