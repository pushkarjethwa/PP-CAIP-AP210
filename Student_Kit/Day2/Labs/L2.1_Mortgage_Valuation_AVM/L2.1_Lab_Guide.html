Automated valuation model (AVM) for mortgage collateral

Build, regularise and stress-test a linear-regression AVM on 200k UAE property valuations — and prove it beats the valuer’s rule of thumb.

Objective & scenario alignment

Horizon Gulf Bank (HGB) is a fictional UAE retail bank with 250,000 customers. Its Board approved Project MIZAN — an AI decisioning platform covering credit, fraud, collateral valuation, cash logistics, customer retention and document automation. You are a junior ML practitioner in the MIZAN squad.

The mortgage desk wants an AVM to (a) pre-screen top-up requests and (b) revalue the whole collateral book every quarter for IFRS 9 LTV monitoring. Model Risk will only approve a model that is interpretable (coefficients), accurate (median error within ±10%), and stable over time. A linear model is therefore the right first choice.

Progressive architecture: reuses the ColumnTransformer pattern you built in Lab L1.2; the AVM is served alongside other MIZAN models on Day 5.

By the end of this lab you will be able to

  • Detect and remove data-entry outliers with a robust, domain-based rule
  • Build strong baselines and regression metrics (MAE, RMSE, R², MAPE, % within ±10%)
  • Fit and interpret simple and multiple linear regression (coefficients, intercept, residuals)
  • Use a log-transformed target and explain coefficients as % effects
  • Tune Ridge and Lasso with cross-validation; read a coefficient path
  • Perform residual and segment-level error analysis, and an out-of-time stress test
AIP-210 exam link

AIP-210 Building linear regression models: OLS, coefficients and intercept, residuals, MAE/MSE/RMSE/R², Ridge (L2) vs Lasso (L1), regularisation strength, iterative optimisation. Know that Lasso performs feature selection by driving coefficients to exactly zero.

1Load, sanity-check and remove data-entry errors 10 min

Why this matters

Valuation feeds contain keying errors (an extra zero, a missing zero). A ×10 error on a AED 2M villa is a AED 18M squared-error disaster that drags every coefficient. We detect them with a domain-relative rule: price-per-sqft compared with the community median.

Your tasks

  1. Load with valuation_date parsed. Compute psf = valuation / size.
  2. Compute psf_ratio = psf ÷ median psf of the same community and property type.
  3. Flag rows with ratio outside [0.25, 4] as entry_error; show 5 examples and remove them into val.
Checkpoint — you should see

≈ 300 entry errors (0.15%) with ratios ≈ 0.1 or ≈ 10 — the classic extra/missing zero.

Think like a practitioner

Why is a relative rule better than a global z-score on price here? (Emirates Hills villas are legitimately 10× an Ajman studio)

2Split and build strong baselines 10 min

Why this matters

We hold out 15% as a test set that is touched once at the end. Then we measure two baselines on validation: the median and the valuer rule (community psf × size). The rule is computed from training rows only — otherwise it leaks.

Your tasks

  1. Add months_since_2018. Split 70/15/15 into train, valid, test (random, random_state=RNG).
  2. Write reg_metrics(y, p) returning MAE, RMSE, R², MAPE and within_10pct (share of predictions within ±10%).
  3. Evaluate both baselines on valid into a results DataFrame.
Checkpoint — you should see

Median baseline R² ≈ 0 (MAPE ≈ 90%). Valuer rule: R² ≈ 0.89, MAPE ≈ 18%, only ≈ 33% within ±10%. This is the bar to beat.

💡 Hint

np.mean(boolean_array) gives the share of True values.

3Simple linear regression — read the coefficients 10 min

Why this matters

With one feature the model is a line: price = intercept + slope × size. Reading the slope in AED per sqft and plotting residuals against fitted values reveals the two problems with modelling raw prices: heteroskedasticity and negative predictions.

Your tasks

  1. Fit LinearRegression on size_sqft → valuation_aed (train). Print intercept and slope.
  2. Plot residuals vs fitted values on a 5k-row validation sample.
  3. Count negative predictions on validation.
Checkpoint — you should see

Slope ≈ AED 700–1,000 per sqft; residuals fan out as price grows; R² well below the valuer rule.

Think like a practitioner

What does the intercept mean physically here? Is a non-zero price for a 0-sqft property a problem?

4Multiple linear regression with a log target 15 min

Why this matters

Property prices are multiplicative: a sea view adds a percentage, not a fixed AED amount. Modelling log(price) with log(size) makes the relationship linear, stabilises the variance and makes coefficients read as % effects. TransformedTargetRegressor applies log for training and exp for predictions automatically.

Your tasks

  1. Build a ColumnTransformer: log-size + numerics (median impute, scale), a SplineTransformer on months_since_2018 (market cycle), one-hot for community/type/view/emirate, passthrough flags.
  2. Fit LinearRegression on raw price and a TransformedTargetRegressor(LinearRegression, func=np.log, inverse_func=np.exp); compare validation metrics.
  3. Print the % effect of each view_* coefficient: exp(coef) − 1.
Checkpoint — you should see

Log-target model: R² ≈ 0.97+, MAPE ≈ 9%, ≈ 60%+ within ±10% — clearly beats the valuer rule. Sea/Marina view ≈ +20–30% vs Road view.

💡 Hint

Coefficients on one-hot columns are relative to the average of the encoded group because we did not drop a reference column; compare views against each other.

Think like a practitioner

The raw-target model has a decent R² but a terrible MAPE. Why do the two metrics disagree? (R² is dominated by expensive properties)

5Regularisation: Ridge vs Lasso with cross-validated alpha 15 min

Why this matters

With ~60 correlated features, coefficients can become unstable. Ridge (L2) shrinks them smoothly; Lasso (L1) can set them to exactly zero — automatic feature selection. The strength alpha is a hyperparameter, chosen by 5-fold CV on the training set.

Your tasks

  1. Tune Ridge alpha ∈ logspace(-3, 3) and Lasso alpha ∈ logspace(-5, -1) with GridSearchCV (5-fold, scoring neg MAE) — both with log target.
  2. Report best alpha and validation metrics for each.
  3. Plot the Lasso coefficient path (|coef| count > 0 vs alpha).
Checkpoint — you should see

CV picks the smallest alphas and Ridge/Lasso tie with plain OLS (≈ 9.2% MAPE): with 140k rows and ~60 features there is little variance to remove. The Lasso path shows features dropping to zero as alpha grows.

💡 Hint

Parameter names inside nested objects use double underscores: regressor__m__alpha.

Think like a practitioner

If regularisation does not improve accuracy here, why might Model Risk still prefer the Lasso model? (parsimony, stability, fewer inputs to monitor)

6Final test evaluation and segment error analysis 12 min

Why this matters

Overall metrics hide pockets of failure. A bank cares whether the AVM is reliable for villas in Palm Jumeirah, not just on average. We evaluate on the untouched test set once, then break the error down by segment and inspect the worst cases.

Your tasks

  1. Choose the best model by validation MAE as final; compute test metrics.
  2. Table of MAPE and within-10% by emirate and by property_type.
  3. Plot actual vs predicted (log-log) and show the 5 largest absolute % errors.
Checkpoint — you should see

Test MAPE ≈ 9%, R² ≈ 0.97. Errors are fairly even across segments; the worst cases are unusual properties (very large, very high floor or old).

Think like a practitioner

A 9% error on a AED 1.5M apartment is AED 135k. For which business decision is that acceptable (portfolio LTV) and for which is it not (individual purchase price)?

7Out-of-time stress test and model hand-off 13 min

Why this matters

Random splits let the model see every month of the market. In production it values tomorrow’s properties. Training on 2018–2024 and testing on 2025 shows how fast the AVM ages — the input to its recalibration policy.

Your tasks

  1. Refit the chosen pipeline on rows before 2025-01-01; predict 2025 rows.
  2. Report OOT MAPE and median signed error (bias) by quarter.
  3. Save the final (random-split) pipeline and a metrics JSON to CKPT.
Checkpoint — you should see

OOT MAPE is a little worse than the random-split test, with a systematic bias (the spline cannot extrapolate the 2025 market) — evidence for quarterly recalibration.

Think like a practitioner

Write the one-paragraph model limitation statement for the model card: what drifts, how you detect it, how often you refit.

Stretch challenges

  1. Add an interaction between emirate and the time spline (Dubai and Abu Dhabi cycles differ). Does OOT bias improve?
  2. Replace the linear model with HistGradientBoostingRegressor on the same features. Is the accuracy gain worth the loss of coefficient interpretability?
  3. Build a prediction interval with quantile regression (QuantileRegressor on a sample) — valuers report ranges, not points.

Troubleshooting

  • Lasso ConvergenceWarning → raise max_iter or increase alpha; features are already scaled.
  • GridSearch is slow → set n_jobs=-1, or tune on a 60k-row sample of train.
  • display not defined → you are not in Jupyter; use print.
