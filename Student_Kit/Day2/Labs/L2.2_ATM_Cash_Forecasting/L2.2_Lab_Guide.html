ATM cash-demand forecasting — univariate, multivariate and quantile

Objective & scenario alignment

Horizon Gulf Bank (HGB) is a fictional UAE retail bank with 250,000 customers. Its Board approved Project MIZAN — an AI decisioning platform covering credit, fraud, collateral valuation, cash logistics, customer retention and document automation. You are a junior ML practitioner in the MIZAN squad.

The Cash Operations team loads each ATM once a week. Today they use last month’s average — causing ≈ 4 emergency replenishments a week and ≈ AED 40M of idle cash. You will deliver (a) a 4-week network forecast for treasury funding and (b) a 7-day-ahead per-ATM forecast with a P90 upper bound for the loading plan.

Progressive architecture: your cleaned network series is the input to the LSTM in Lab L4.2.

By the end of this lab you will be able to

  • Handle outage gaps in a panel time series without corrupting features
  • Build naive, seasonal-naive and moving-average baselines; use MAE / MAPE / WAPE
  • Fit univariate Holt-Winters and multivariate SARIMAX with exogenous calendar drivers
  • Engineer leakage-safe lag features for a direct 7-day horizon across 60 ATMs
  • Validate with TimeSeriesSplit; compare a linear model and gradient boosting
  • Produce quantile (P90) forecasts and measure coverage — the operational number
AIP-210 exam link

AIP-210 Building forecasting models: univariate vs multivariate forecasting, lag features, moving averages, seasonality, forecast evaluation, future-data leakage. Exam tip: a model whose features use information after the forecast origin is invalid regardless of its accuracy.

1Load the panel and repair outage gaps 10 min

Why this matters

On outage days the ATM was down, so the recorded value is missing — not zero demand. If we leave NaNs, every lag that touches them becomes NaN; if we fill with 0, the model learns fake crashes. We interpolate for feature-building but exclude outage days from evaluation.

Your tasks

  1. Load and sort by atm_id, date. Add was_outage (1 where withdrawal is missing).
  2. Interpolate withdrawal_aed within each ATM (time-based, both directions).
  3. Build net: network total per date with the calendar columns; save it to CKPT/atm_network_daily.parquet.
Checkpoint — you should see

≈ 900 outage ATM-days; the network series is complete with 2,496 days.

Think like a practitioner

An ATM was down for 3 days. When it comes back, demand is higher than normal (pent-up). How could you model that?

2Univariate baselines on a 4-week horizon 10 min

Why this matters

Treasury needs a 28-day network forecast. Forecast origin: 31 Aug 2025; horizon: 1–28 Sep. Baselines define the bar: naive (repeat the last day), seasonal naive (repeat the last week) and the 28-day moving average. WAPE (Σ|error| / Σ actual) is the metric cash teams use because it weights big days properly.

Your tasks

  1. Split net at 2025-08-31 into hist and fut (next 28 days).
  2. Write fc_metrics(actual, forecast) → MAE, MAPE, WAPE.
  3. Compute the three baselines and store them in fc (a DataFrame indexed like fut).
Checkpoint — you should see

Surprise: the 28-day moving average wins (WAPE ≈ 9–10%) and seasonal naive is worst (≈ 17%) — the last observed week (25–31 Aug) was a payday week, so repeating it over-forecasts all of September. Baselines must be chosen with the calendar in mind.

💡 Hint

np.tile(arr, 4) repeats an array end-to-end four times.

3Holt-Winters (univariate) and SARIMAX with calendar drivers (multivariate) 18 min

Why this matters

Holt-Winters models level + trend + weekly seasonality from the series alone. SARIMAX adds autoregressive structure and exogenous regressors — payday, pre-Eid, holiday and weekend flags that are known in advance. That is multivariate forecasting with only legitimate future information. We train on the post-2022 regime (relevant history).

Your tasks

  1. Fit ExponentialSmoothing(trend='add', seasonal='mul', seasonal_periods=7) on hist from 2022-01-01; forecast 28 days.
  2. Fit SARIMAX(order=(1,0,1), seasonal_order=(1,1,1,7)) on log withdrawals with exogenous calendar flags; forecast with the future flags.
  3. Compare all five methods and plot actual vs the two best.
Checkpoint — you should see

SARIMAX with calendar drivers wins by a mile (WAPE ≈ 2%). Holt-Winters (≈ 15%) cannot know the payday ramp is coming. The payday coefficient ≈ 0.23 on the log scale ⇒ ≈ +26% withdrawals in the payday window; pre-Eid ≈ +35%.

💡 Hint

SARIMAX.forecast(steps, exog=...) needs the exogenous values for every future step — that’s why they must be known in advance.

Think like a practitioner

Temperature is in the data too. Should it be an exogenous driver for a 28-day forecast? (only if you have a reliable 28-day weather forecast)

4Per-ATM features for a direct 7-day-ahead model 15 min

Why this matters

Loading plans are per ATM, a week ahead. In a direct strategy every feature must be available 7 days before the target date, so the freshest lag is lag 7. We add calendar features known in advance, the location type, and a day-of-week × weekend-regime interaction so one model handles both weekend regimes.

Your tasks

  1. On atm (grouped by ATM) create lag_7, lag_14, lag_21, lag_28, roll7_lag7 (mean of days t-13…t-7) and roll28_lag7 — all with shift(7) first.
  2. Add dow_regime = day-name + regime, and month.
  3. Write an assert that proves no feature uses data later than t-7 (e.g. compare lag_7 with the value 7 rows earlier).
  4. Keep rows from 2021-07-01; target y = log1p(withdrawal_aed); log-transform lags too.
Checkpoint — you should see

≈ 90k training and 7.2k test ATM-days (last 120 days).

Think like a practitioner

Why is temperature_c excluded from the per-ATM model even though it is in the file?

5Linear vs gradient-boosting forecasters with TimeSeriesSplit 15 min

Why this matters

We compare an interpretable Ridge model with a HistGradientBoosting model (a tree ensemble you’ll study tomorrow) on identical features, tuning on TimeSeriesSplit folds of the training period, and benchmark both against the per-ATM seasonal naive (lag_7).

Your tasks

  1. Build a ColumnTransformer (scale lags; one-hot dow_regime, location_type, atm_id, month; passthrough calendar flags).
  2. Tune Ridge alpha with GridSearchCV(cv=TimeSeriesSplit(4)).
  3. Fit HistGradientBoostingRegressor(max_iter=400, learning_rate=0.06) on the same matrix.
  4. On test days that were not outages, report WAPE for seasonal naive, Ridge and HGB, overall and by location_type.
Checkpoint — you should see

Both ML models halve the error of seasonal naive (≈ 17% → ≈ 8% WAPE; HGB slightly ahead). Industrial-area ATMs, driven by payday, improve the most (≈ 27% → ≈ 8–12%).

💡 Hint

Because panel is sorted by date, TimeSeriesSplit on row order is a forward split in time.

Think like a practitioner

Ridge and HGB are close. Which would you put in front of the Cash Operations manager, and why?

6P90 quantile forecasts → the loading plan 12 min

Why this matters

Running out of cash is far more expensive than carrying a little extra. Operations therefore load to an upper quantile. A model trained with quantile loss at 0.9 should be exceeded on only ~10% of days. Coverage, not accuracy, is the KPI here.

Your tasks

  1. Fit HistGradientBoostingRegressor(loss='quantile', quantile=0.9) on the same pipeline.
  2. On non-outage test days compute coverage = share of days with actual ≤ P90, and the average buffer (P90 − actual) in AED.
  3. Save ev columns (date, atm_id, actual, ridge, hgb, p90) to CKPT/atm_forecasts_test.parquet.
Checkpoint — you should see

Coverage close to 90% overall (≈ 87–92%), with some variation by location type.

Think like a practitioner

Coverage is 84% for airports. Is that a model problem or a data problem? What would you change first?

Stretch challenges

  1. Hierarchical coherence: do the 60 per-ATM forecasts sum to the network SARIMAX forecast? Reconcile them proportionally.
  2. Add a rolling-origin evaluation for SARIMAX: 6 origins one month apart. How stable is WAPE?
  3. Estimate the business value: if an emergency CIT visit costs AED 1,800 and idle cash costs 5% p.a., compare P80, P90 and P95 policies.

Troubleshooting

  • SARIMAX is slow / ConvergenceWarning → it is fitting ~1,300 points with weekly seasonality; 20–60 s is normal. Warnings are usually harmless here.
  • asfreq('D') creates NaNs → you dropped dates during aggregation; check net.index.is_monotonic_increasing.
  • Ridge GridSearch slow → atm_id one-hot adds 60 columns; that’s fine, but reduce alpha grid if needed.
